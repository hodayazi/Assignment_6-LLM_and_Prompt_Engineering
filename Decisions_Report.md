# Decisions Report### 1\. Overview of the Two Improvements TestedIn the optimization process, two distinct retrieval-augmentation adjustments were designed and measured over the frozen evaluation dataset of 20 questions:* **Improvement 1: Expansion of Context Window (k=10)**  * **What was done:** Increased the number of retrieved context documents from the default k=5 up to k=10.  * **Result/Score:** Groundedness score remained stagnant at **0.133** (Net Lift: **+0.000**).  * **Why it did not help:** While a larger candidate pool provides more raw information, the strict instructions in `GROUNDED\\\_SYSTEM` coupled with semantic matching limits prevented the LLM from synthesizing or mapping slightly abstract questions to the retrieved papers without explicit examples.* **Improvement 2: Relaxed Few-Shot Prompting (`GROUNDED\\\_SYSTEM\\\_V2` with previous k=5)**  * **What was done:** Softened the ultra-conservative "I don't know" rule to allow answers when context topics (e.g., U-Net, fractures, segmentation) matched. Explicit positive/negative few-shot examples were also provided.  * **Result/Score:** Groundedness score increased from **0.133** to **0.267** (Net Lift: **+0.133**).  * **Why it worked:** The few-shot demonstration corrected the over-refusal behavior by explicitly teaching the LLM the distinction between completely irrelevant out-of-scope tasks (such as general knowledge questions about France or TikTok) and domain-specific questions that could be answered from partial matching.\---### 2\. Failure Analysis: Why Does the Model Still Fail on Certain Questions?Despite a +0.133 net lift, the overall groundedness remains relatively low (under 0.300). Upon closer inspection, the failed questions share unique traits:1. **Semantic Gaps in Un-chunked Retrieval:** Abstracts were not chunked. Long, dense abstracts embedded with `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` may suffer from semantic dilution, causing the bi-encoder to rank irrelevant abstracts higher than the precise papers holding the answers.2. **Synonym and Specific Terminology Mismatch:** Questions asking about highly specific techniques (e.g., supervised contrastive learning or specific model architectures) fail to surface the exact relevant abstracts because the vector database retrieves papers containing general domain terms ("radiograph", "bone") rather than the precise methodological matches.3. **Stringent Citation Extraction:** The evaluation proxy relies strictly on finding clean arXiv ID formats in the output. If the model answers correctly but fails to format the citations exactly, or if the relevant citation is missing from the retrieved top-k pool, the score registers as `0.0`.\---### 3\. Future Work with Larger Token/Budget ConstraintsIf a larger token and budget balance were available, several high-impact upgrades could be implemented:* **Neural Reranking (Bi-Encoder + Cross-Encoder):** Retrieve a broad candidate pool (k=30) to guarantee recall, and then use a computationally expensive Cross-Encoder model (e.g., `cohere-rerank` or `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`) to sort the most relevant context snippets to the top.* **Advanced Chunking with Parent-Child Relationships:** Split abstracts into overlapping sentences (children) for highly granular semantic matching while feeding the entire abstract (parent) as context to the LLM to preserve cohesion.* **Larger LLM Reasoning Backends:** Leverage top-tier reasoning engines (such as OpenAI's full models or Claude 3.5 Sonnet) with dynamic prompt structures and chain-of-thought verification to analyze complex relations between the papers and synthesize grounded answers.
